## Making Claims

### When to File a Claim
File a claim when:
- Covered incident occurs (accident, theft, damage, illness)
- You need to use your insurance benefits
- Immediate action needed for emergency coverage
- Within the claim reporting timeframe

### Types of Claims

#### Auto Insurance Claims
- Accident damage
- Theft
- Vandalism
- Third-party liability
- Windscreen damage
- Natural disasters

#### Home/Property Insurance Claims
- Fire damage
- Theft/burglary
- Water damage
- Natural disasters
- Vandalism
- Liability claims

#### Health Insurance Claims
- Medical treatments
- Hospital admissions
- Surgeries
- Diagnostic tests
- Pharmacy/medications
- Emergency care

#### Life Insurance Claims
- Death benefit
- Disability benefits
- Critical illness
- Maturity benefits

### Before Filing a Claim

#### Immediate Actions
1. **Ensure Safety**: Take care of injuries first
2. **Secure Scene**: Prevent further damage if possible
3. **Document Everything**: Photos, videos, notes
4. **Collect Information**: 
   - Other parties involved
   - Witnesses
   - Police reports (if applicable)
   - Medical reports (if applicable)
5. **Don't Admit Fault**: State facts only
6. **Notify Authorities**: Police, fire department, etc.
7. **Contact Insurance**: Report within required timeframe

#### Documents to Gather
- **Proof of Loss**: Photos, videos, receipts
- **Police Report**: For theft, accidents, crimes
- **Medical Reports**: For injury claims
- **Repair Estimates**: From approved vendors
- **Receipts**: For stolen/damaged items
- **Witness Statements**: Contact information
- **Incident Report**: From work, building, etc.

### Filing a Claim

Access via **Dashboard > Claims** or policy page

#### Step 1: Start Claim
- Click "File New Claim"
- Select the relevant policy
- Claim form opens

#### Step 2: Incident Details
- **Date of Incident**: When it happened
- **Time**: Approximate time
- **Location**: Where it occurred (address, GPS)
- **Type of Incident**: Select category
  - Accident
  - Theft
  - Damage
  - Illness/Injury
  - Other
- **Description**: Detailed account of what happened
  - Circumstances
  - How it occurred
  - Who was involved
  - Extent of damage/injury

#### Step 3: Parties Involved
If applicable, add details of:
- **Other Drivers**: For auto accidents
- **Third Parties**: Anyone else involved
- **Witnesses**: Names and contacts
- **Contact Information**: Phone, email
- **Insurance Details**: Their insurance info

#### Step 4: Damage/Loss Details
- **Items Affected**: What was damaged/lost
- **Estimated Value**: Cost to repair/replace
- **Age of Items**: Purchase date
- **Original Cost**: What you paid
- **Current Condition**: Before incident
- **Attachments**: Photos, receipts, valuations

#### Step 5: Medical Information
For injury claims:
- **Injuries Sustained**: Type and severity
- **Medical Treatment**: Where and when treated
- **Healthcare Provider**: Doctor, hospital details
- **Diagnosis**: Medical diagnosis
- **Treatment Plan**: Ongoing care needed
- **Work Impact**: Days missed
- **Medical Bills**: Attach invoices

#### Step 6: Police/Authority Reports
- **Report Filed**: Yes/No
- **Report Number**: Reference number
- **Police Station**: Where filed
- **Officer Name**: Reporting officer
- **Upload Report**: Attach copy

#### Step 7: Supporting Documents
Upload all relevant documents:
- Photos of damage (multiple angles)
- Video evidence
- Police report
- Medical reports and bills
- Repair estimates (2-3 quotes)
- Purchase receipts
- Proof of ownership
- Witness statements
- Any other supporting evidence

#### Step 8: Bank Details
For claim payment:
- Bank name
- Account number
- Account name
- Branch or routing number
- Swift code (if international)

#### Step 9: Declaration
- Read terms and conditions
- Confirm information accuracy
- Declare truthfulness
- Understand fraud consequences
- Authorize investigation
- Sign electronically

#### Step 10: Submit
- Review all information
- Check for completeness
- Note claim reference number
- Click "Submit Claim"
- Receive confirmation email

- Payment processed

2. **Understand Reasons**: Why claim was denied/settled at that amount

### Missed Payments

#### Grace Period
- Usually 15-30 days after due date
- Coverage continues during grace period
- Pay before grace period ends

#### Policy Lapse
If not paid within grace period:
- Policy lapses/cancels
- Coverage stops
- May incur reinstatement fee
- Could affect future insurability
- Claims not honored during lapse

#### Reinstatement

1. Pay all outstanding premiums
2. Pay reinstatement fee (if applicable)
3. May need to reapply/re-underwrite
4. Provide current information
5. Waiting period may apply
6. Coverage reinstated upon approval

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Policy Questions

**Q: How do I know what insurance I need?**
A: Assess your risks, assets, and legal requirements. Consider auto (if you drive), home/renters (if you own/rent), health, and life insurance.

**Q: What's the difference between comprehensive and third-party insurance?**
A: Comprehensive covers damage to your property and others'. Third-party only covers damage you cause to others.

**Q: Can I have multiple insurance policies?**
A: Yes, you can have different types (auto, home, health, life) or multiple policies of the same type.

**Q: How often should I review my insurance?**
A: At least annually, or when major life changes occur (moving, new car, marriage, children, etc.).

**Q: Can I cancel my policy anytime?**
A: Usually yes, but you may face penalties or only get partial refund. Check your policy terms.

### Premium Questions

**Q: Why did my premium increase at renewal?**
A: Factors include claims history, age, inflation, risk reassessment, or general rate increases.

**Q: How can I lower my premium?**
A: Increase deductible, improve risk profile, bundle policies, remove unnecessary coverage, ask about discounts.

**Q: Do I get a discount for paying annually?**
A: Most insurers offer a discount for annual payment vs. monthly payments.

**Q: What happens if I miss a premium payment?**
A: You have a grace period (usually 15-30 days). After that, policy may lapse and coverage stops.

### Claims Questions

**Q: How long does a claim take?**
A: Simple claims: 1-2 weeks. Complex claims: 4-8 weeks or longer. Timeframe varies by claim type and complexity.

**Q: Will my premium increase if I make a claim?**
A: It may, especially for at-fault auto claims. Small claims might not be worth losing your no-claims bonus.

**Q: Can I choose my own repair shop?**
A: Depends on policy. Some require approved vendors, others allow choice but may have limits.

**Q: What if my claim is denied?**
A: Review the reason, appeal if you disagree, provide additional evidence, or escalate to ombudsman.

**Q: Do I need to pay the deductible to file a claim?**
A: No, you file first. The deductible is deducted from the settlement amount.

### Coverage Questions

**Q: Am I covered if I lend my car to someone?**
A: Typically yes, if they have your permission and a valid license. Check your specific policy.

**Q: Does my home insurance cover flooding?**
A: Not always. Flood coverage may be separate or excluded. Check your policy.

**Q: Are my belongings covered outside my home?**
A: Many home/renters policies have some coverage for personal property away from home.

**Q: What's the difference between replacement cost and actual cash value?**
A: Replacement cost pays to replace with new. Actual cash value accounts for depreciation.

### Technical Questions

**Q: How do I access my policy documents?**
A: Navigate to My Policies, select the policy, and download from the Documents section.

**Q: Can I manage multiple policies in one account?**
A: Yes, all your policies appear in one dashboard.

**Q: I forgot my policy number. How do I find it?**
A: Log in to the portal and view My Policies section, or check your email for policy documents.

**Q: Is my information secure?**
A: Yes, we use encryption and follow data protection regulations. Never share your login credentials.

### Complaint Process

2. Speak with supervisor if needed
3. File formal complaint through portal
4. Allow 10-15 days for resolution
5. Escalate to ombudsman if unresolved
6. Consider legal options as last resort
